Why you can’t trust the 98% at face value

This batch is 88% positive. A model that output “POSITIVE” on every review, never reading a word, would still score 88%.

Naive baseline: 88%  →  Model: 98%

So the real, honest margin is +10 points — that is what the model actually earns.

2 of 100

What the evidence says

good vs. bad

Strong at clear sentiment

Perfect on negative reviews (12/12) and on unambiguous positives. Crisp, obviously-sentiment one-liners (“Great gift”, “Do not buy!”) are handled correctly.

Never cries wolf on negatives

Zero false positives — no genuine negative review was called positive. That zero is the strongest signal in the matrix.

Drifts on lukewarm 4-star reviews

Its only 2 errors are 4-star reviews with complaint-y prose. The model judges tone, not the 4/5 rating boundary, so mild-but-satisfied reviews can look negative.

Small negative sample

Only 12 negatives in 100. “100%” on negatives is encouraging but not statistically settled — a larger, harder negative set would give real confidence.
