At a glance

Judged on a balanced sample — one third of each class — the model is 78.7% accurate overall. It is strong at the two extremes (positive 94%, negative 92%) and improved in the middle: it now keeps 25 of 50 true 3-star reviews (50%) as NEUTRAL, up from 18%.

How to read this

The three classes

  • POSITIVE ratings of 4–5 — reviews that praise, recommend, or express satisfaction.
  • NEUTRAL ratings of 3 — neither clearly positive nor negative.
  • NEGATIVE ratings of 1–2 — complaints, warnings, or dissatisfaction.

Confusion matrix

  • Rows are the correct class (from the star rating); columns are what the model predicted.
  • Correct answers sit on the diagonal. Everything off the diagonal is a mistake.
  • A balanced sample means every class is equally tested, so the classes are directly comparable.

NEUTRAL is still the weakest class — but much improved

True 3-star reviews are kept as NEUTRAL 50% of the time now (up from 18%). Of 50, the model keeps 25 as NEUTRAL, moves 21 to NEGATIVE, and 4 to POSITIVE. The remaining misses still lean toward NEGATIVE — 3-star reviews often carry a reservation that the model reads as a complaint.

25 kept NEUTRAL  ·  21 → NEGATIVE  ·  4 → POSITIVE

What the pattern adds up to

The model still over-uses NEGATIVE, but far less than before. It now calls 68 reviews NEGATIVE (50 truly are) and 31 NEUTRAL (50 truly are). The middle class is where most remaining errors live, but the gap has narrowed substantially.
